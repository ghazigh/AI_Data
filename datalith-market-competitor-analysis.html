01

Executive summary

7% of enterprises say their data is completely ready for AI Cloudera / HBR · Mar 2026
57% have traced a confidently wrong agent answer to missing business context VB Pulse · Jun 2026 · n=101
81% of repeat-failure firms plan to buy a context platform within 12 months vs. 32% of never-failed firms
$600M combined ARR of Fivetran + dbt Labs, merged 1 Jun 2026 80,000 data teams · $10B+ valuation

The problem Datalith targets is real, large, and now analyst-validated as a budget line rather than a thesis. Gartner elevated semantic layers and knowledge graphs to critical infrastructure at its Data & Analytics Summit 2026 and predicts more than 50% of AI agent systems will use context graphs by 2028. Only 25% of enterprises have a governed context layer in production today; 34% are building one. The serviceable market for a cross-platform agentic context layer is credibly $1.5–3.5B by 2028 Assumptions.

The critical competitive insight is that the window Datalith aims at has already been entered by the platform vendors it planned to out-manoeuvre. Snowflake shipped Cortex Sense and Horizon Context in June 2026; Databricks shipped Unity Catalog Business Semantics and Genie Ontology across April–June; Microsoft shipped Fabric IQ Ontology with public MCP endpoints. Most dangerously, Fivetran and dbt Labs completed their merger on 1 June 2026 — branded, in their own words, "the data infrastructure for trusted AI agents" — occupying the exact neutral cross-platform seat Datalith's moat argument depends on.

The two highest-ROI opportunities are to reposition the free wedge from a static readiness audit to continuous agent-answer failure attribution — the one pain 57% of enterprises have already felt and for which no cross-vendor product exists — and to sell MCP governance as a CISO-budget purchase, converting Datalith's own stated deal-blocker into its buyer while 30+ MCP CVEs in two months make the pain acute. The single biggest risk is not competition but capital and timing asymmetry: a $4.0M seed against $5–15M category norms, entering 6–12 months after the incumbents shipped, with a Series A milestone that is internally inconsistent with the company's own conversion assumption. Datalith's thesis is right and its category is funded; its defensibility must be re-argued on measurement, cross-vendor unstructured coverage, and agent-side governance — not on auto-ontology, which is now table stakes.

The four questions this paper answers
Question 01

What problem does Datalith solve, and how is that problem evolving?

Enterprises deploy agents faster than their data can support them — now measured rather than asserted. The evolution that matters: Gartner turned semantic layers into a budget line in 2026, so Datalith no longer has to create the category. It also no longer gets to define it alone, and every incumbent now markets in its vocabulary.

Question 02

Can it win against current competitors?

Not on the differentiation currently written down. Estate-wide auto-ontology was commoditised in a single quarter by Databricks, Snowflake and Microsoft. Datalith can win on ground none of them hold — cross-vendor span, unstructured and SaaS-object coverage, readiness measurement, and agent-side governance. Those must become the headline.

Question 03

What is the single biggest risk to its position?

Not the warehouses. Fivetran + dbt Labs — ~$600M ARR, 80,000 data teams, $10B+ — now hold the neutral cross-platform seat, selling to Datalith's exact ICP in Datalith's exact words. Compounding it, the Open Semantic Interchange standard is turning semantic portability from a product into a checkbox.

Question 04

What is the highest-ROI opportunity in the next 12 months?

Retime the wedge from the strategy deck to the incident. A static readiness audit sells to planning cycles; failure attribution sells to the 81% of repeat-failure firms already in market. Platform vendors optimise forward accuracy and structurally cannot attribute failures in data they cannot see.

2.3 · Growth drivers
D1

Measured, repeated agent failure attributed to data — not models

57% of enterprises traced a confidently wrong agent answer to missing or inconsistent business context; 31% saw it more than once (VB Pulse, Jun 2026). Gartner separately predicts organisations will abandon 60% of AI projects lacking AI-ready data through 2026, and over 40% of agentic AI projects by end-2027. Concretely: an agent that reads a stale dim_customer and quotes the wrong contract value produces an incident with a name, a date and an owner.

Why it matters — this is the trigger event the whole GTM depends on, and it is now empirically frequent rather than anecdotal. Critically, 81% of repeat-failure firms plan to buy within 12 months vs. 32% of never-failed firms — the buying signal is the incident, not the strategy deck. Datalith's current wedge is timed to the strategy deck.
D2

Analyst legitimisation converting semantics into a budget line

At Gartner D&A Summit 2026, semantic layers and knowledge graphs were positioned as foundational infrastructure alongside data platforms and cybersecurity; Gartner predicts over 50% of AI agent systems will leverage context graphs by 2028, and an IDC study (May 2026) found grounding AI in knowledge graphs cut hallucination rates by an average of 44%.

Why it matters — budget existence is the difference between evangelical and competitive selling. Datalith no longer has to create the category — but every incumbent's marketing now uses its vocabulary.
D3

MCP adoption outrunning MCP governance

28% of Fortune 500 have deployed MCP; 41% of software organisations run MCP servers in production; SDK downloads reached ~97M/month by early 2026; Gartner projects 75% of API gateway vendors will ship MCP features by end-2026. Against that: 30+ CVEs were filed against MCP servers, clients and infrastructure between January and February 2026, independent scans find a majority of public MCP servers carry exploitable risk, and only a small fraction use OAuth by default.

Why it matters — the most under-exploited driver in the plan. Datalith's GTM names the CISO as the blocker to be neutralised. The market data says the CISO is a buyer with an urgent, unserved, budgeted problem — and Gateway is already designed for it.
D4

Regulatory forcing function with a dated deadline

EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations became enforceable 2 Aug 2026 — six days after this report — carrying fines to €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover. High-risk Annex III obligations were deferred to 2 Dec 2027. The Act applies extraterritorially to any system placed on the EU market.

Why it matters — "prove what data your agent touched and why it answered that way" becomes a legal artefact on a known date. The Dec 2027 deadline aligns almost exactly with Datalith's v2 platform timeline — a rare case where a startup's roadmap and a regulatory deadline coincide.
D5

Consolidation pressure and fragmentation fatigue

Fivetran + dbt Labs merged 1 Jun 2026, following Fivetran's acquisitions of Census and Tobiko Data. Microsoft Fabric, Databricks Lakeflow and Snowflake native ingestion converge on the same promise: fewer vendors, unified billing. Steven Dickens (HyperFRAME) summarised the buyer mood as "data teams are exhausted by fragmentation fatigue."

Why it matters — this cuts against Datalith. A new standalone vendor is a cost in a consolidating market. Datalith must be sold as a replacement for N hand-rolled MCP servers — a net vendor reduction — not as an addition.

2.6 · Trends shaping the next 2–3 years

01 · Auto-generated ontologies become table stakes

Snowflake Cortex Sense builds context from query history, metadata and BI definitions; Databricks Genie Ontology maintains a living graph with authority-weighted definitions; Microsoft Fabric IQ does the same for Fabric.

So what — "we auto-construct your knowledge graph" has a shelf life of roughly 12 months as a claim.

02 · Semantics become an open standard, not a product

OSI: Apache-2.0, YAML-based, launched by Snowflake, Salesforce, dbt Labs, BlackRock and RelationalAI; v1.0 published 27 Jan 2026; Phase 2 targets native support in 50+ platforms through Q4 2026.

So what — a direct attack on the neutral-semantic-layer vendor position. Switzerland is being replaced by a treaty.

03 · Runtime context ownership is the strategic prize

Michael Ni (Constellation Research): "Whoever controls runtime context controls the AI decision layer for enterprise data." Snowflake has explicitly split curation (Horizon Context, human) from runtime ingestion (Cortex Sense, agent).

So what — the durable position is the serving path at query time, not the catalog. Datalith Gateway is the strategically correct module — and is sequenced fourth of six.

04 · Multi-vendor context is the expected end state

VB Pulse buyer guidance explicitly advises expecting multi-vendor solutions rather than a single winner; Kevin Petrie (BARC) flags unstructured and document context as the standing gap.

So what — Datalith needn't displace Snowflake or Databricks; it must span them and cover what they don't. A smaller, winnable claim than "the Agentic Data Center."

05 · Measurement of readiness remains unclaimed

Readiness assessment in 2026 is dominated by consultancy frameworks (PwC Ready Assess, boutique 5- and 7-dimension scorecards). No vendor was found selling a continuously computed, product-generated readiness score.

So what — the Agent-Readiness Score is Datalith's most genuinely unoccupied ground.

04

SWOT

Strengths

Evidence-backed
Thesis validated by independent, current, third-party data. 7% data-ready (Cloudera/HBR); 57% incident rate and 57% 12-month purchase intent (VB Pulse); Gartner D&A 2026; >50% of agent systems on context graphs by 2028. The founding premise needs no defending in a pitch. Diligence time is spent on execution, which is where a pre-product company wants the conversation.
Time-to-value and price attack incumbent economics precisely. 48-hour first ARS vs. 5–6 month implementations; $30K/yr Growth tier vs. ~$170–198K/yr entry. Quantified, checkable, and aimed at the incumbents' most-complained-about attribute in G2 reviews.
The measurement layer is genuinely unoccupied. No product-generated, continuously computed readiness score found among any competitor; the space is held by consultancy frameworks. The one asset in the plan with no incumbent — also the cheapest to build and the most viral.
Read-only, cross-platform, in-VPC architecture matches where the market is going. 86% of APAC organisations on hybrid AI; 77% factoring supplier nationality; EU AI Act extraterritoriality. A sovereignty and security asset in the two regions where hyperscaler bundling is weakest — an advantage the current positioning does not claim.
Protocol-open design is correctly timed. MCP at 28% Fortune 500 penetration, ~97M SDK downloads/month, 75% of API gateway vendors shipping MCP by end-2026. Building MCP-native in 2026 is neither early nor late; "the graph is the asset, wire formats are commodities" is the right hedge against protocol churn.

Weaknesses

Benchmarked
The headline differentiator has been commoditised since the strategy was written. Cortex Sense (Jun 2026), Genie Ontology (Apr–Jun 2026) and Fabric IQ Ontology all ship auto-built ontologies with published or claimed benchmark gains. Every discovery call will hit "our warehouse already does this." Differentiation must move to cross-vendor span, unstructured coverage, measurement and agent-side governance — none of which currently lead the narrative.
The most dangerous competitor is absent from the competitive analysis. Fivetran and dbt are cited as analogies for neutrality; they merged on 1 Jun 2026 into a ~$600M ARR, $10B+ company holding that seat. An investor who knows the space finds this in five minutes. Unaddressed, it reads as stale research or avoidance — the more damaging inference in diligence.
Under-capitalised relative to category norms and entry timing. $4.0M ask vs. $5–15M typical agentic-AI seeds (the plan's own benchmark); competitors hold $65M, $206M and $765M; ~$600K must cover SOC 2 Type I+II and in-VPC packaging. $4M buys ~18 months to build six modules, 150+ connectors, a graph, a gateway and SOC 2 Type II against vendors who shipped last quarter. Either the raise or the scope has to change.
Series A milestones are internally inconsistent. The plan assumes 8–12% audit→paid conversion and sets "100+ audits → 25 paying accounts." 100 audits at 10% yields ~10 paid; reaching 25 requires ~210–310 audits. A diligence-grade arithmetic error in the central funnel. Fix by raising the audit target to ~250, defending a higher conversion rate, or lowering the milestone.
Six modules is too much surface for a seed team. Connect (150+ connectors), Graph, Topics, Ready, Flows and Gateway — funded with six engineers. Fivetran needed years and hundreds of engineers for the connector estate alone. Shipping six modules thinly against vendors shipping one deeply is the most common way this category of company fails.

Opportunities

Linked to drivers
Incident-triggered wedge: agent-answer failure attribution. 57% have had confident-wrong incidents, 31% repeatedly; 81% of repeat-failure firms plan to buy within 12 months vs. 32% of never-failed firms. The buying trigger is the incident, not the audit. A product answering "which data caused this wrong answer" sells into an open wound, is inherently cross-vendor, and produces the graph as a by-product.
MCP governance as a security-budget purchase. 28% Fortune 500 MCP deployment; 30+ CVEs Jan–Feb 2026; majority of public servers exploitable; security the leading adoption blocker. Converts the CISO from named blocker into buyer, unlocks a faster-moving budget, and shortens the cycle. Gateway already has the required design — it needs repositioning, not rebuilding.
EU AI Act evidence layer. Article 50 enforceable 2 Aug 2026 (€15M / 3% turnover); Annex III high-risk Dec 2027; extraterritorial reach. A compliance-shaped buying reason with a fixed date, strongest in the region with the lowest agent-scaling saturation and therefore the fewest incumbent context layers installed.
Federating platform-native context layers via OSI. OSI v1.0 published 27 Jan 2026, Apache 2.0; Phase 2 targeting 50+ platforms by Q4 2026. Instead of competing with Snowflake, Databricks and Fabric semantics, import and reconcile them. OSI turns three rivals into three data sources at near-zero cost.
The unstructured and SaaS-object gap. BARC names unstructured/document context as the standing market gap; warehouse-native layers and dbt semantics are both table- and metric-shaped. Tickets, wikis, contracts, transcripts and images are where no platform incumbent reaches and where Glean does not serve open agents — the narrowest defensible territory in the analysis.

Threats

Ranked by severity
Bundling removes the addressable market faster than competition takes it. Cortex Sense, Genie Ontology and Fabric IQ are included in existing platform contracts with zero procurement friction; buyers report fragmentation fatigue. Every account that consolidates leaves the SAM without ever running a bake-off. This is market shrinkage, not a win-rate problem — and it is absent from the internal risk table.
The neutrality moat is being standardised away. OSI: Apache 2.0, founded by Snowflake, Salesforce, dbt, BlackRock and RelationalAI, v1.0 Jan 2026, 50+ platforms targeted by Q4 2026. If semantic portability becomes a free open standard, neutrality stops being a product and becomes a checkbox. Datalith must add a layer above portable semantics.
A well-capitalised adjacent player makes one product decision. Glean: $300M ARR, +89% YoY, $7.2B valuation, already holds the SaaS/unstructured connector estate and a permissions-aware index. Glean opening its graph to third-party agents would take Datalith's most defensible territory with connectors it already runs. A quarters-scale risk, not a years-scale one.
Consolidation raises the bar for standalone vendors. Fivetran+dbt merged; Fivetran previously acquired Census and Tobiko; Microsoft, Databricks and Snowflake all converging on end-to-end. A seed-stage standalone must show it removes vendors. The six-module platform pitch reads as adding one.
MCP security backlash could slow the whole adoption curve. 30+ CVEs in two months; majority of public servers exploitable; under 4% of RSA Conference submissions framed MCP as opportunity. A serious breach would slow agent deployment industry-wide and compress demand. Partially hedged — it also strengthens the governance sale. Net effect ambiguous, magnitude high.

Market challenges

C1

Bundled substitutes with zero procurement friction

Example — a 1,500-person fintech with 70% of analytical data in Snowflake evaluates Datalith. Its Snowflake AE offers Cortex Sense: already in the contract, benchmarked at 86.3% accuracy, no new vendor, no new security review, no new invoice.

Impact — Datalith must win on the 30% of data Snowflake cannot see, which reframes the deal from platform purchase to gap-filler and compresses ACV well below the $60–120K assumed internally. Assumptions — no win/loss data exists for a pre-product company.

C2

Security review is the long pole — and it lengthens under agentic scrutiny

Example — read-only credentials across 10 production systems plus an MCP endpoint serving agents triggers a full vendor security assessment. With 30+ MCP CVEs filed in early 2026 and MCP security the leading reported blocker, review committees are hardening exactly this surface.

Impact — the "connect in an afternoon, report in 48 hours" wedge is achievable technically but not procedurally at enterprises. SOC 2 Type II requires an observation period that will not complete inside the Y1 revenue plan. This gates the enterprise segment for most of year one.

C3

Payback-period scepticism inherited from the incumbents

Example — the buyer's last governance purchase was Collibra or Alation, with a 5–6 month implementation and a 21–25 month payback. That buyer now discounts every time-to-value claim in the category by default.

Impact — Datalith's 48-hour claim is its best asset and its least believable one. It must be demonstrated live in the first call — which makes a self-serve, credential-less demo path a gating requirement rather than a nice-to-have.

C4

Connector coverage is a capital problem disguised as an engineering problem

Example — the product strategy promises 150+ connectors. Fivetran built ~700 over a decade with far more capital; Glean's connector estate underpins a $7.2B valuation.

Impact — with six engineers and ~18 months, 150+ connectors is not reachable at production quality. The 90-day plan's five connectors is the realistic figure, and the website and deck should be aligned to it before diligence. Over-claiming coverage is the fastest way to lose a technical champion.

C5

Talent competition at the exact skill intersection

Example — knowledge-graph plus identity-resolution plus agent-runtime engineers are being hired against by Databricks, Snowflake, Glean, Palantir and DataHub — all better capitalised, several with public liquidity.

Impact — four senior engineers on graph and infrastructure inside a $4M raise implies below-market compensation offset by equity, lengthening hiring and raising founder-dependency risk. Assumptions — no compensation benchmarking was performed; direct job-posting analysis was not conducted Missing.

6.2 · Blue ocean
Blue ocean 01

The Agent Accountability Layer

Reposition from "data readiness" to the system of record for what agents were told, what they did with it, and who is answerable.

Why underserved
Every incumbent optimises the forward path — making agents more accurate. Nobody owns the backward path: attribution when an agent is wrong, evidence when a regulator asks, per-agent identity when security asks. The three constituencies with this pain — the CDO after an incident, the CISO facing MCP CVEs, the compliance owner facing Article 50 and Annex III — currently buy nothing that serves all three, because the product does not exist
What it requires
Gateway resequenced from module six to module one, so every agent query produces the audit log and attribution trace as exhaust · lineage from served context back to source field, scoped to serving paths rather than full-estate ontology (a materially smaller build) · evidence export mapped to EU AI Act articles and SOC 2 / ISO 42001 control language · no requirement to out-build Snowflake or Databricks on ontology quality — this wins without winning the ontology race
Trend fit
Directly on Constellation's "whoever controls runtime context controls the AI decision layer"; consistent with buyers expecting multi-vendor context; converts MCP's security crisis from an adoption headwind into the reason to buy
Blue ocean 02

The Agent-Readiness Index

Not a product — a published, annual, cross-industry benchmark with per-company private scoring.

Why underserved
Categories are captured by whoever owns the measure — Net Promoter, DORA, the Gartner MQ itself. Readiness measurement today is consultancy-shaped and non-comparable. DataHub has already proven the mechanism works in this exact market: its State of Context Management 2026 is cited as authority by third parties, including in Datalith's own research. That is the position Datalith can still take, because no one has claimed the number — only the narrative
What it requires
ARS methodology published openly and defensibly (dimensions, weights, computation) so non-customers can cite it · 150–250 audits to produce a statistically presentable first index — which also repairs the funnel arithmetic gap by making the audit target the primary Y1 goal · an analyst-relations and content motion rather than an enterprise sales motion, materially cheaper and better matched to a $4M raise
Trend fit
Rides Gartner's 2026 elevation of semantic layers to critical infrastructure — executives now need a number to report against a budget line they have just been told is non-negotiable — and operationalises the 7%-ready statistic every vendor quotes but none can compute per company
